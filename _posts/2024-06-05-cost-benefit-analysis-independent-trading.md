## Quick answer

The lowest quoted part price is not automatically the lowest procurement cost. For critical generator parts, compare each route using the same technical specification, evidence requirements, logistics scope, payment terms, and outage risk. Independent sourcing is an alternative route—not a substitute for engineering approval or a reason to accept unclear documentation.

## Compare like with like

Before comparing suppliers, create one buying specification. It should state the exact part number and revision, condition, quantity, required documents, packaging standard, Incoterms, destination, and required arrival date. Without a common specification, two quotations may describe different items or obligations.

## Five costs that belong in the decision

### 1. Purchase and inspection cost

Include the quoted price, any incoming inspection work, documentation review, and the cost of resolving an item that does not meet the agreed specification.

### 2. Logistics and customs cost

Compare freight, insurance, export paperwork, destination handling, customs obligations, and preservation requirements. Confirm who is responsible for each step.

### 3. Lead-time certainty

Ask whether the item is physically available, what evidence supports that statement, and what event would change the release date. “Lead time” is valuable only when its assumptions are clear.

### 4. Downtime exposure

The relevant business question is often: what is the cost if this component is not available when the approved work needs it? Record that risk separately from the item price.

### 5. Technical and commercial risk

Consider compatibility approval, warranty expectations, supplier documents, return terms, payment security, and the ability to investigate a discrepancy. These controls protect both the plant and the supplier.

## A simple comparison table

| Decision point | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Technical match | Is the part number, revision, condition, and application confirmed? |
| Evidence | What photos, labels, documents, and traceability are available? |
| Release | Is the quoted quantity physically available and ready for release? |
| Delivery | Who owns freight, insurance, customs, and destination clearance? |
| Risk | What happens if the item is late or fails incoming inspection? |

## Frequently asked questions

### Does independent sourcing mean lower quality?

Not necessarily. Quality depends on the specific item, its condition, evidence, and the buyer’s acceptance process. Evaluate the offer, not the label “independent.”

### When is independent sourcing a sensible option?

When the plant has a clearly defined requirement and wants to compare viable supply routes under its normal engineering and procurement controls.

### What information should a buyer retain?

Keep the request, approved specification, supplier documents, received-item inspection, and installation outcome. It improves the next procurement decision.
