In a multi-cylinder industrial gas engine, achieving balanced combustion across every single cylinder is vital for mechanical longevity. Monitoring Exhaust Gas Temperatures (EGT) provides plant operators with real-time insight into internal combustion dynamics. When one cylinder runs significantly hotter or cooler than the rest, it signals an underlying operational imbalance that requires immediate investigation.

At **Mega Solutions UAE**, we work with technical teams troubleshooting combustion efficiency. Here is how to approach EGT imbalances systematically.

### 1. What Causes EGT Imbalances?
An abnormal spike or drop in a specific cylinder's exhaust temperature typically points to one of a few critical issues:
* **Fuel Delivery Discrepancies:** A partially blocked gas admission valve or faulty port injector delivering inconsistent fuel volumes to a single cylinder.
* **Ignition Misfires:** Weak spark energy or a degraded ignition coil causing incomplete combustion, which lowers that cylinder's EGT while sending unburnt fuel into the exhaust manifold.
* **Valvetrain Wear:** Leaking exhaust valves or incorrect valve lash clearances altering chamber compression and thermal release.

### 2. Diagnostic and Corrective Action Steps
1. **Review Thermocouple Accuracy:** Ensure the EGT sensor itself isn't fouled or failing before tearing down mechanical parts.
2. **Inspect Ignition and Fuel Rails:** Check spark plugs and gas metering valves on the affected cylinder. Replacing worn ignition components often restores thermal equilibrium instantly.
