Maintaining a precise air-fuel ratio (AFR) is the holy grail of modern gas engine management. Whether you are running a lean-burn Jenbacher unit or an independent co-generation setup, even minor fluctuations in fuel delivery can throw your combustion dynamics completely off balance. 

At the heart of this control loop sits the **gas dosing valve**. When these valves respond sluggishly or lose calibration, your plant experiences engine knocking, unstable load response, and painful drops in power efficiency.

### Why Air-Fuel Ratio Drift Occurs

Gas dosing valves operate under constant mechanical cycling and exposure to pipeline impurities. Over time, varnish build-up from heavy hydrocarbons or particulate matter can restrict the internal butterfly or plunger movement. 

When the actuator commands a specific fuel volume but the mechanical valve sticks by a fraction of a millimeter, your engine management system enters a continuous cycle of over-correction. Plant operators commonly refer to this as "hunting"—where the engine speed and load fluctuate rhythmically, driving your control room operators crazy.

### Key Strategies to Stabilize Load Response

* **Periodic Actuator Calibration:** Ensure your electronic actuators and feedback potentiometers are calibrated during routine maintenance intervals to prevent lag between the ECM command and physical valve movement.
* **Upstream Gas Filtration:** Install high-efficiency coalescing filters upstream of your dosing valves to trap moisture and fine aerosols before they coat internal valve seats.
* **Keep High-Spec Replacements on Hand:** When a valve actuator experiences internal stepper motor failure or feedback drift, field-repairing it is rarely practical. Having a drop-in replacement gas dosing valve on your warehouse shelf ensures minimal disruption during emergency turnarounds.
