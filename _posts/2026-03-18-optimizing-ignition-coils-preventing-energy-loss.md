When an industrial gas engine suffers from intermittent power loss or difficult cylinder lighting, technicians often swap out the spark plugs first. But what happens when brand-new plugs don't fix the misfire? More often than not, the root cause lies deeper in the electrical circuit: **degraded ignition coils**.

In continuous-duty co-generation plants, ignition coils are subjected to intense thermal cycling and high electromagnetic stress. Over time, internal epoxy insulation breaks down, leading to micro-cracks that allow high-voltage current to arc directly to the grounded cylinder head instead of jumping the plug gap.

### Recognizing Coil Breakdown and Secondary Leakage

* **Weak Spark Energy:** A failing coil cannot deliver the secondary voltage required to fire under high-load cylinder pressures, resulting in a soft or delayed spark.
* **Insulation Tracking:** Look for faint carbon tracking lines or white powdery corrosion along the secondary output tower of the coil block.
* **Harness Degradation:** High engine bay temperatures harden rubber boots, allowing moisture and oil vapor to creep into the primary connector pins.

### Best Practices for Ignition Reliability

1. **Replace Boots with Every Coil Swap:** Always install fresh silicone spark plug extension boots whenever you replace a coil. Reusing old, heat-brittle boots virtually guarantees secondary voltage leakage.
2. **Grounding Integrity Check:** Ensure your ignition module grounding straps are clean, tight, and free of paint or oxidation to prevent high-frequency electrical noise from corrupting your ECU timing signals.
